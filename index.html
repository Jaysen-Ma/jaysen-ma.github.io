Research Projects

What post-training changes: reasoning gains and safety side effects of ES vs RL

github.com/Jaysen-Ma/es-vs-rl-post-training

I compared evolution strategies (ES) with reinforcement learning using GRPO to study how maths-only post-training changes language models. Using Qwen2.5-1.5B and 7B, I examined mathematical performance, general knowledge and moral judgement. At 7B, GRPO achieved stronger first-attempt accuracy, while ES retained more of the base model’s ability to solve problems over samplings.

Both methods preserved general knowledge performance, but ES significantly reduced scores on some ETHICS moral judgement tasks at both model sizes. These exploratory results suggest that improvements in maths can accompany changes in behaviour beyond the training task, raising questions about safety and alignment as evolutionary training scales up. The study uses single training runs and unequal training budgets, so it does not isolate the effect of the training method.

Exploring variation in the exponential metalaw of physics

github.com/Jaysen-Ma/physics-metalaw

Scientific domains favour different mathematical operators and expression structures. I investigate whether these preferences can be characterised quantitatively and used to guide symbolic regression.

Building on earlier work on exponential operator-frequency distributions, I revisit the lower fitted decay rate found in inflationary models and explore variation across physics branches. The study combines expanded equation collections, comparisons with modified-gravity models, bootstrap uncertainty estimates and sensitivity analyses. It also examines the relationship between decay rate and complexity for individual expressions.

The longer-term aim is to turn these patterns into domain-dependent priors or structural penalties that could help symbolic regression search more efficiently for equations that explain the data. The current work explores the statistical patterns and their limitations; their usefulness for improving equation recovery remains to be tested.

New inflationary potentials with evolutionary algorithms

MPhys thesis, University of Oxford

I searched for new models of cosmic inflation with evolutionary algorithms. I generated candidate functional forms for the inflationary potential and used genetic algorithms to search each form's coefficient space, finding a viable class that combines natural and arctan inflation (viable meaning its predictions are consistent with cosmological observations). I then studied the structure of its viable parameter bands and their predictions.

Next, I applied exhaustive symbolic regression to a viable potential to generate new functional forms with similar shapes. Searching their coefficient spaces turned up further viable inflationary potentials.

CoT Injection: red-teaming Muse Glimmer's reasoning channel

github.com/Jaysen-Ma/muse-cot-injection

Muse Glimmer 30B, an open-weights reasoning model by Meta, deliberates in a private reasoning channel before it answers. I tested what happens when something other than the model (e.g. an orchestrator) writes into that channel. An auxiliary model (Gemma 4 31B-it) wrote a fabricated chain of thought in the target's own style, concluding that a harmful request could be answered, and this was injected as the model's (Muse Glimmer) own reasoning. On 200 StrongREJECT prompts, compliance rose from 0.5% with the plain prompt to 69.5% with reasoning-channel injection. The identical text pasted into an ordinary user message gave only 1.0%, so the attack depends on write access to the reasoning channel. The risk sits with systems that construct a model's reasoning.

I then trained a LoRA defence on 104 corrective examples written by the target model itself. Attack success fell to less than 1% both overall and on two held-out harm categories, from 76.3% on those categories before the defence. When harmless reasoning was injected into harmless prompts, the model answered only 7 out of 20, so part of that drop reflects a reflex against any injected reasoning. Training the LoRA on mixed data rather than refusal-only data could improve this.
